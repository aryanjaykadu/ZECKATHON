## 1. Industry Analysis

### Market Structure & Value Chain
The Indian capital markets infrastructure sector functions as a quasi-monopolistic, highly regulated network structured across three essential layers:
1. **Trading Layer (Exchanges — BSE, NSE)**:
   - **Market Structure**: Virtual duopoly between NSE (~93% cash market share, ~82% equity options notional share) and BSE (~7% cash, ~18% options notional share).
   - **Value Chain**: Order routing from brokers $\rightarrow$ Exchange matching engine $\rightarrow$ Colocation/data dissemination $\rightarrow$ Clearing corporation (ICCL/NSCCL) settlement.
2. **Custody & Settlement Layer (Depositories — CDSL, NSDL)**:
   - **Market Structure**: Duopoly where CDSL commands ~77% of total active demat accounts and NSDL holds ~70% of total demat custody value (institutional bias).
   - **Value Chain**: Issuer listing $\rightarrow$ Depository Participant (DP) onboarding $\rightarrow$ Demat holding/annual maintenance $\rightarrow$ Pledge/unpledge & secondary market debit settlement.
3. **Servicing Layer (Registrars & Transfer Agents — CAMS, KFintech)**:
   - **Market Structure**: Rigid duopoly servicing ~100% of mutual fund AUM (CAMS ~68%, KFintech ~32%).
   - **Value Chain**: Investor KYC & folio creation $\rightarrow$ AMC transaction processing $\rightarrow$ Dividend/corporate action reconciliation $\rightarrow$ Distributor commissions & regulatory filings.

### Regulatory Licences, Switching Costs & Moat Durability
- **Licensing & Regulatory Barriers**: SEBI’s Market Infrastructure Institutions (MII) regulations enforce severe net-worth floors, continuous capital adequacy, and strict ownership caps (individual entities capped at 5%, non-MIIs capped at 15%). Obtaining new exchange or depository licenses is virtually impossible.
- **Switching Costs**:
  - *AMCs $\rightarrow$ RTAs*: Prohibitive. Shifting millions of mutual fund investor folios, banking integrations, and distributor trails entails severe operational, regulatory, and systemic risk. Churn across top AMCs is near-zero.
  - *Brokers $\rightarrow$ Exchanges*: Low switching costs technically, but deep liquidity network effects dominate.
- **Network Effects**:
  - Liquidity begets liquidity on exchanges: market makers and algorithmic high-frequency traders concentrate orders on contracts with minimum bid-ask spreads. BSE’s revitalization of the Sensex/Bankex derivative contracts broke NSE's multi-year monopoly by offering complementary contract expiries.
- **Pricing Power**:
  - Exchanges have historically enjoyed pricing power through slab-based structures.
  - Depositories enjoy strong pricing power on transaction debits (₹4.25–₹5.50 per debit) with zero marginal cost per digital debit.

### Impact of SEBI F&O Curbs & Fee Rationalisation ("True-to-Label")
1. **Direct Impacts**:
   - *SEBI Expiry Curbs (Nov 2024)*: Limitation to one index expiry per exchange per week (Sensex for BSE, Nifty for NSE) directly eliminated redundant mid-week contracts (Bankex), contracting weekly turnover frequency.
   - *Contract Lot Size Increase*: Minimum contract size raised to ₹15–₹20 lakhs curbs low-ticket retail speculation.
   - *"True-to-Label" Fee Circular*: Prohibits exchanges from charging slab-based volume fee structures while brokers charge flat retail rates. Exchanges forced to move to uniform transaction tariffs.
2. **Second-Order Effects**:
   - Institutional and proprietary trading firms retain capital in the single weekly expiry, significantly deepening liquidity on Sensex expiry days (Fridays).
   - As retail options turnover moderated, trading transitioned to higher-quality institutional volumes, mitigating fee decline through higher average order value.
   - Depository pledge/unpledge volumes remain insulated as equity cash delivery volumes and SIP folios continue steady compounding.
